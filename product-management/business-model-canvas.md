# Business Model Canvas (BMC)

The Business Model Canvas is a one-page strategic management tool that lays out all nine structural components of a business model side by side, so you can see how they connect — and where they don't — at a glance. It matters because it's the artifact that converts a vision statement and market research into an actual, testable business logic: who you serve, what you give them, and how the whole thing makes money.

## When to use

Fill out a BMC after you've done vision-setting, market/competitor analysis (4Cs), and initial customer interviews — it's explicitly positioned in the deck as step 4 of a sequence: **vision → competitive analysis → interview customers to test hypotheses → set the business model.** Use it whenever you're validating a new business idea, evaluating a pivot, or need one page that aligns founders/investors/team on how the business actually works (not just what it sells).

## How it works

The canvas has 9 blocks. Each block below is framed as the question(s) to ask, the types/categories to keep in mind, and how to get the answer.

1. **Customer Segments** — For whom are we creating value? Who are our most important customers? One segment or more? *Get the answer from:* previous research, value-based segmentation. *Market types to keep in mind:* Mass market (everyone — e.g. phones), Niche market (a specific group with unique needs — e.g. sportswear), Diversified (different products for different groups — e.g. Uber Bus vs. Uber Cars), Multi-sided platform (two dependent groups — e.g. Uber driver and rider).

2. **Value Propositions** — What value do we deliver to the customer? Which customer problems are we helping solve? Which needs are we satisfying? *Get the answer from:* competitive analysis (customer value), customer research/interviews, competitor gaps. *Value types to keep in mind:* Newness (something no one offered before), Performance (faster/easier), Customization, Saving time and effort, Brand/status, Cost reduction, Accessibility.

3. **Channels** ("touch points") — How will the company communicate with and reach customers? What channels deliver the product? How do we raise awareness? How do we help customers evaluate the value proposition (samples, reviews, influencers)? How do we provide post-purchase support? *Get the answer from:* competitive analysis (communication). *Channel types:* Owned channels (website, social media, in-house sales, own stores) vs. Partner channels (partner-owned sites, wholesale distribution, retail).

4. **Customer Relationships** — What type of relationship does each customer segment expect us to establish and maintain? *Get the answer from:* competitive analysis (communication). *Relationship types:* Personal assistance (human interaction via phone/email), Self-service (no relationship, customer helps themselves), Automated services (automated processes standing in for personal assistance), Communities (customers help each other), Co-creation (customer involved in designing the product — e.g. YouTube creators).

5. **Revenue Streams** — How do we earn revenue from each value proposition? *Get the answer from:* cost analysis. *Revenue types:* Transaction-based (one-time payment), Usage fee (pay for use), Subscription fee (recurring, regular use), Brokerage/commission fees (intermediary between parties), Advertising (charge to advertise on the platform).

6. **Key Activities** — What activities/tasks must be completed to fulfill the business purpose? These should focus on fulfilling the value proposition, reaching segments, maintaining relationships, and generating revenue. *Get the answer from:* analyzing the value proposition. *Activity types:* Production (designing, manufacturing, delivering at scale/quality), Platform/network (creating and maintaining platforms).

7. **Key Resources** — Which resources/main inputs are needed to carry out the key activities and create the value proposition? *Get the answer from:* analyzing the key activity. *Resource types:* Human (employees), Financial (cash, credit lines), Physical (equipment, inventory, buildings), Technology.

8. **Key Partnerships** — Who are our key partners/suppliers? Which key resources are we acquiring from partners? Which key activities do partners perform? *Get the answer from:* optimization/economy of scale (operating at larger scale), reduction of risk and uncertainty (splitting responsibility), acquisition of resources/activities you don't have access to. *Partnership types:* Strategic alliance (between non-competitors), Cooperation (strategic partnership between partners), Buyer-supplier relationships (ensuring reliable supply).

9. **Cost Structure** — What are the most important costs inherent in the business model? Which key resources/activities are most expensive? Is the business cost-driven (low price, low cost structure, high automation, high outsourcing) or value-driven (focus on value creation, premium)? *Get the answer from:* analyzing the key activity. *Cost types:* Fixed costs (rent, salary), Variable costs (commission, packaging), Economies of scale.

The nine blocks read left-to-right/top-to-bottom as a causal chain: Value Proposition sits at the center; Customer Segments, Relationships, and Channels (right side) describe how you deliver and capture that value externally; Key Partners, Activities, and Resources (left side) describe what it takes internally to produce it; Cost Structure and Revenue Streams (bottom) tie the whole thing to the P&L.

## Example

**Uber, worked through all 9 blocks:**

- **Key Partners:** Drivers (independent contractors), payment processors (financial institution partnerships)
- **Key Activities:** Development, matching algorithm, quality control, marketing
- **Key Resources:** Technology platform (the mobile app and backend systems)
- **Value Propositions:** Convenience (on-demand transportation at the push of a button), Reliability (predictable, timely transportation); for drivers: extra income
- **Customer Relationships:** Technology interaction (automated, tech-driven customer interactions through the app), Customer support (in-app support and issue resolution)
- **Channels:** Mobile app (primary platform for riders and drivers), Website (alternative access point)
- **Customer Segments:** Riders — individuals who need transportation
- **Cost Structure:** Technology development, driver acquisition and retention, marketing and promotion
- **Revenue Streams:** Ride fees (per completed trip), Surge pricing (additional charges during high demand)

Notice the multi-sided platform detail from Customer Segments (drivers and riders) surfaces again in Key Partners (drivers) and Value Propositions (a distinct value prop *for drivers*, separate from riders) — the canvas forces you to notice when a business has more than one customer to design for.

## Applying it for a client

Run the BMC as a live, one-page whiteboard exercise with the client's founding team in the room — not a document you fill in solo and present back. Start from Value Proposition (the center) and work outward; if the team can't agree on that block, stop and go back to customer research rather than filling in the other 8 blocks around an unclear center. For a branding agency positioning a client, the BMC is diagnostic before it's creative: a repositioning brief built without first checking Cost Structure and Revenue Streams against Value Proposition risks selling a story the unit economics can't support. For a clinic or service business, the Multi-sided-platform and Co-creation nuances rarely apply — spend the saved time doing the Key Partnerships block properly (insurance providers, referring physicians, equipment suppliers), which is usually underdeveloped in first-draft canvases from non-tech clients.

## Watch-outs

- The canvas describes a business model, not a strategy — it tells you the pieces and how they should connect, not which pieces to build first (that's [product-roadmap.md](product-roadmap.md) and [prioritization-techniques.md](prioritization-techniques.md)).
- Blocks are interdependent — changing Customer Segments cascades into Value Proposition, Channels, and Relationships. Treat a BMC edit as a full re-check of the canvas, not an isolated patch.
- A common failure mode: filling in Revenue Streams and Cost Structure with wishful numbers before validating Value Proposition and Customer Segments with real interviews. The canvas is only as good as the research feeding it (see [mvp-customer-validation.md](mvp-customer-validation.md)).
