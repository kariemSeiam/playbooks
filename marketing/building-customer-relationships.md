# Building Customer Relationships

The closing move of the marketing process: after a customer has been correctly targeted (STP) and won over (4Ps), the business's job shifts to keeping them — because a strong, long-term relationship increases profit and is one of the few durable advantages competitors can't easily copy.

## When to use

As soon as a business has its first customers, not after it "has time." Also reach for this whenever a client is spending heavily on acquisition but not retention — the numbers below are the fastest way to show a client why that's usually a mistake.

## How it works

**Why it matters — two benefit categories, both with hard numbers attached:**

### Customer retention

- 90% of customers prefer doing business with a brand they already know and trust rather than trying a new product.
- Maintaining the attention of repeat customers costs 5 times less than attracting new customers.
- Repeat customers spend significantly more money — specifically 67% more — than first-time buyers.

### Improving loyalty

- Leads to increased sales: customers keep coming back repeatedly, so the business can spend less on marketing campaigns as word of mouth starts selling for it.
- Enhances engagement: customers who truly love the brand and product are more likely to respond to email surveys, attend brand-related events, join loyalty programs, and support the business on social media through likes, shares, tags, and comments.

**Practical tips for building the relationship:**

- Don't just think about the current sale; consider future sales.
- Ask customers about their preferences.
- Present the value of the product (not just the transaction) to the customer.
- Offer deals specifically for returning customers.
- Be empathetic — apply the customer-profile thinking from the Value Proposition Canvas (understand actual pains/gains, don't guess).
- Communicate in a way that suits each person, rather than one uniform script for everyone.

## Example

A subscription meal-kit business in Cairo notices most marketing budget goes to Facebook ads for new customers, while churned subscribers get no follow-up. Applying the retention math above: acquiring that same revenue from existing subscribers would cost roughly a fifth as much, and a retained subscriber's average order value is already meaningfully higher than a first-time buyer's. The business reallocates a portion of ad spend into: a returning-customer discount code, a short preference survey after the third delivery (to catch dissatisfaction before cancellation), and a referral perk that turns loyal subscribers into an acquisition channel via word of mouth — directly compounding the "increased sales, lower marketing spend" loyalty benefit.

## Applying it for a client

When a client's growth plan is 100% acquisition-focused, use the 90% / 5x / 67% figures directly in the room — they're concrete enough to shift budget conversations without needing custom data. Then translate the six tips into an actual retention checklist for their business: what does "ask about preferences" look like concretely for them (a survey? a support-ticket tag?), what does a "deal for returning customers" look like given their margin structure, and which channel fits "communicate in a way that suits each person" (WhatsApp vs. email vs. SMS, given the actual customer base). Pair this with the Value Proposition Canvas (see [value-proposition-canvas.md](value-proposition-canvas.md)) — empathy here isn't a soft skill, it's reusing the same Pains/Gains/Jobs-to-be-done data already gathered for STP.

## 2024→2026 shift

The practical tips above — communicate in a way that suits each person, present value rather than just the transaction — are increasingly executed through AI rather than a human agent making judgment calls for every customer. Two changes worth naming to a client:

- **AI-powered personalization at scale.** Instead of segment-level personalization (one message per customer segment), generative AI now lets a business produce a distinct message, offer, or content variant per individual customer, driven by that customer's own behavior and preference data — the "communicate in a way that suits each person" tip from the source material is no longer aspirational, it's operationally achievable for businesses with the data and tooling to support it.
- **Agentic content pipelines within brand guardrails.** Platforms like Adobe GenStudio and HubSpot's AI tooling now let a marketing team define brand rules once (tone, visual identity, approved claims) and have an AI pipeline generate and adapt retention content — win-back emails, loyalty offers, re-engagement messages — within those guardrails automatically, rather than a human rewriting each variant by hand.

This is emerging-but-credible: the tooling is real and already shipping in production platforms, but the guardrail layer (making sure AI-personalized messaging doesn't drift off-brand or make an unauthorized claim at scale) is the actual hard part, and most organizations are still early in getting it right. When advising a client on this, treat "we personalize with AI" the same way you'd treat any new retention tactic — ask for the actual retention-rate evidence, not just the fact that the tooling is in place.

## Watch-outs

- Loyalty programs and retention tactics bolted onto a weak value proposition don't fix churn — if the core product isn't solving the customer's actual pain (see [value-proposition-canvas.md](value-proposition-canvas.md)), no amount of relationship-building tactics will retain them.
- The 90%/5x/67% figures are general benchmarks, not guarantees for every business or market — use them to make the retention argument, but validate retention economics with the client's own numbers (their actual acquisition cost and repeat-purchase rate) before sizing an investment.
- "Communicate in a way that suits each person" requires knowing the person — this only works if the segmentation and customer-profile work (STP, Value Proposition Canvas) was actually done, not skipped.
