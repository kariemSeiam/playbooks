# PESTEL Analysis

A six-lens scan of the macro-environment a business operates in — the forces no single company controls but that shape what's possible for all of them. It's the "external" half of market-conditions analysis, done systematically instead of by whichever risk happens to come to mind.

## When to use

Whenever a client is entering a new market or geography, planning for the medium-to-long term, or has been blindsided by an "external shock" (currency move, new law, supply disruption) and wants a structured way to scan for the next one. Pairs naturally with SWOT (see [swot-analysis.md](swot-analysis.md)) as the external-environment complement to that internal/external tool.

## How it works

Six categories, each with the concrete sub-factors to actually check (not just the letter):

- **Political** — fiscal policy, government activity, conflicts / foreign aid, taxes.
- **Economical** — GDP, employment rate, exchange rate, inflation, income level.
- **Social** — demographic variables, cultural factors, religion, lifestyle, education level.
- **Technological** — technological access, infrastructure, research, technology trends.
- **Environmental** — environmental policies, recycling, consumption trends, production processes, natural risks.
- **Legal** — wages, rights, job security, regulations, control of marketing (advertising law, consumer protection).

Work through all six even briefly — the value of PESTEL is forcing you past the one or two factors that are top-of-mind (usually Economic and Political) into categories that are easy to skip (Environmental, Legal) but that regularly blindside businesses that never checked them.

## Example

A ride-hailing startup evaluating expansion into a new Egyptian governorate:
- **Political**: local government stance on ride-hailing licensing.
- **Economical**: local fuel prices and exchange-rate exposure on any imported vehicle parts.
- **Social**: local attitudes toward women using ride-hailing apps alone (relevant to driver/rider mix).
- **Technological**: smartphone and mobile-data penetration in the governorate.
- **Environmental**: fuel-emission regulations, EV-incentive programs.
- **Legal**: transport-sector regulation, data-privacy rules for rider information, advertising restrictions on public transport ads.

## Applying it for a client

Don't run all six categories with equal depth for every client — triage first: ask which 2-3 categories this specific business is actually exposed to (an e-commerce play cares more about Technological + Legal + Economical; a food producer cares more about Environmental + Economical + Political), then go deep only there and note the rest briefly. A PESTEL that treats all six as equally important produces a bloated slide nobody reads; a triaged one produces a short list of the 2-3 risks/opportunities that actually change the plan.

## Watch-outs

- PESTEL identifies factors, it doesn't rank them — always follow up with "which of these actually matters enough to change our strategy," or the exercise becomes an unprioritized list.
- Political and Economic factors move fast in unstable macro environments (very relevant in Egypt/MENA) — a PESTEL from even a few months ago can be stale; check currency and regulatory factors close to the decision date, not just at project kickoff.
- Confusing PESTEL (macro-environment, applies to everyone in the market) with SWOT's Opportunities/Threats (specific to one business's position) — PESTEL factors become Opportunities/Threats only once you've assessed how they specifically affect this business.
