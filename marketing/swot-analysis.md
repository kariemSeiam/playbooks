# SWOT Analysis

A four-quadrant scan that separates what a business controls (internal) from what it doesn't (external), and what helps it from what threatens it. It's the fastest way to get a business's situation on one page before deciding anything strategic.

## When to use

Early in any engagement, as part of "understanding the marketplace" (see [understanding-the-marketplace.md](understanding-the-marketplace.md)), or whenever a client is about to make a strategic bet (enter a new market, launch a product, raise pricing) and needs a structured gut-check rather than a hunch.

## How it works

Four quadrants, split along two axes — internal vs. external, and helpful vs. harmful:

| | Helpful | Harmful |
|---|---|---|
| **Internal** | **Strengths** — characteristics of the business that give it advantages over competitors | **Weaknesses** — characteristics of the business that put it at a disadvantage relative to competitors |
| **External** | **Opportunities** — elements in the external environment that allow the business to formulate and implement strategies to improve performance | **Threats** — elements in the external environment that could endanger the business, its profitability, or its competitive advantage(s) |

The internal/external split is the discipline that matters most: Strengths and Weaknesses must be things the business itself controls (team, product, cost structure, brand); Opportunities and Threats must be things happening in the world around it (regulation, competitor moves, technology shifts) that the business does not control but must respond to. Mixing the two columns (e.g., listing "low brand awareness" as a threat) collapses the tool's usefulness.

## Example

A handmade-soap startup in Alexandria:

- **Strengths**: founder has a chemistry background, all-natural ingredient sourcing already secured.
- **Weaknesses**: no e-commerce presence, single-person production limits volume.
- **Opportunities**: growing consumer interest in natural/organic personal care, rising demand for local Egyptian-made gift products.
- **Threats**: established imported natural-soap brands entering local retail, currency devaluation raising the cost of imported packaging.

## Applying it for a client

Run SWOT as a live 20-minute workshop with the client's team rather than filling it in yourself from the outside — Strengths and Weaknesses in particular need insider knowledge you won't have. Then immediately cross-reference: strategy usually comes from matching Strengths to Opportunities (where can we win fastest), and Weaknesses to Threats (what could actually sink us). A SWOT that's filled in and then filed away has produced nothing; the pairing step is where the actual strategic insight lives.

## Watch-outs

- Generic entries ("weakness: limited budget," "opportunity: growing market") that could apply to any business are a sign the exercise wasn't done with real specificity — push for what's true of *this* business.
- SWOT describes a snapshot in time; it goes stale, especially the Opportunities/Threats columns in fast-moving markets. Don't let a six-month-old SWOT anchor a current decision.
- It's a diagnostic, not a strategy — SWOT tells you what's true, it doesn't tell you what to do. Pair it with PESTEL for the external half and with STP/4Ps for the "what to do about it."
