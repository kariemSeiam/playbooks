# Marketing Strategy Overview

Once the marketplace is understood (market conditions, competitors, customers), marketing strategy is the deliberate act of deciding *whom* to serve and *how*, then translating that decision into concrete, executable choices. It runs on two sequential models: the **STP Model** decides who you serve and what image you occupy in their mind; the **Marketing Mix (4Ps)** decides what you actually do about it — product, price, place, promotion.

## When to use

Immediately after the marketplace has been mapped (see [understanding-the-marketplace.md](understanding-the-marketplace.md) and [competitor-analysis-matrix.md](competitor-analysis-matrix.md)) and before any tactical execution — a channel plan, a price list, an ad campaign. If a client wants to talk tactics before either STP or the 4Ps have been worked through, that's the signal to pull them back a step.

## How it works

Marketing strategy sits at steps 2-3 of the broader 5-step marketing process (see [intro-to-marketing.md](intro-to-marketing.md): Understand Marketplace → **Design the Strategy → Apply the Strategy** → Customer Relationships → Capture Value). The two models map directly onto those two steps:

1. **Design the Strategy — STP Model** (Segmentation, Targeting, Positioning). Answers "whom and how am I serving?" Takes the segments identified in market segmentation (see [market-segmentation-customer-vs-buyer.md](market-segmentation-customer-vs-buyer.md)) and narrows them to one or a few worth targeting, then decides what image the brand should occupy in that target's mind relative to competitors. Full breakdown: [stp-model.md](stp-model.md).

2. **Apply the Strategy — Marketing Mix (4Ps)**. Answers "what do we actually do?" Product, Price, Place, Promotion — the levers a business pulls to deliver on the position chosen in STP. Full breakdown: [marketing-mix-4ps.md](marketing-mix-4ps.md).

The dependency runs one direction: the 4Ps only make sense in light of a chosen STP. A "premium pricing" decision (a 4Ps lever) is only correct if STP positioned the brand as premium in the first place; a "mass-market distribution" decision only makes sense for an undifferentiated targeting strategy. Running the 4Ps without having done STP first produces a set of tactics with no throughline — a business that's priced like a discount player, packaged like a premium one, and advertised like a niche one, because no one decided who it's actually for.

Downstream of both models sits **Building Customer Relationships** (see [building-customer-relationships.md](building-customer-relationships.md)) — the mechanism by which a one-time transaction from a correctly-targeted, correctly-mixed sale becomes a repeat customer and, eventually, the "Capture Value" step of the overall marketing process.

## Example

A social enterprise selling recycled-plastic school supplies has already mapped its market (session-1 work) and knows its segments (eco-conscious urban parents, schools with sustainability mandates, NGOs doing bulk procurement). Marketing strategy work starts here: STP picks the target (schools with sustainability mandates, as the most profitable and accessible segment) and positions the brand ("the school-supplies brand for institutions serious about sustainability," functional + symbolic positioning). Only then does the 4Ps get decided: Product (durable, branded-for-institutions packaging), Price (value-based, justified by the sustainability story), Place (direct B2B sales to procurement offices, not retail shelves), Promotion (case studies and PR aimed at school administrators, not consumer social ads).

## Applying it for a client

Use this note as the table of contents you hand a client (or yourself) before opening either sub-framework: confirm segmentation is already done, then walk STP to a concrete positioning statement, *then* and only then open the 4Ps worksheet. If a client engagement is time-boxed, this sequencing also tells you where to cut: skipping STP to save time is far more costly than skipping depth on one of the 4Ps, because STP is the decision everything else depends on.

## Watch-outs

- The two-model structure implies a strict sequence (STP before 4Ps) that's easy to violate under deadline pressure — resist finalizing a price or a channel plan before the target segment and position are settled, even provisionally.
- Some marketing curricula extend the mix to 7Ps (adding People, Process, Physical Evidence, common for services marketing) — this material covers the classic 4Ps only; note the gap explicitly if advising a services or hospitality client where the extra 3Ps matter.
- Strategy is not "done once" — a repositioning, a new competitor, or a shift in the target segment's needs should send you back through STP before touching the 4Ps again.
