# Community-Led Growth & Influencer Marketing

Two related but distinct channels that both substitute peer or community trust for brand-asserted trust. **Influencer marketing** pays or partners with individual creators who've already built an audience's trust to carry a brand's message to that audience. **Community-led growth** builds a durable space — a forum, a Discord or Slack, a user group, an event series — where customers connect with each other, not just with the brand, turning the customer base itself into an acquisition and retention channel. Influencer marketing is battle-tested with mature benchmarks and agency infrastructure; community-led growth is a newer, less standardized discipline still developing its own playbook and metrics. A consultant needs both because they share the same underlying insight — audiences trust other people more than they trust brands — but require very different operating models, timelines, and success metrics.

## When to use

- **Influencer:** launching a product that needs fast third-party credibility and reach into a niche audience the brand can't build organically in time; a UGC or authenticity need paid ads alone can't produce
- **Community:** a product has genuine power users who want to talk to each other, not just to support, or the business's growth is currently 100% brand-initiated with zero peer-to-peer channel developed
- **Either:** a client's CAC is rising on traditional paid channels and needs a trust-based alternative
- Retention-focused engagements where a product has activation but weak long-term stickiness — community specifically addresses stickiness that content or email alone doesn't

## How it works

**Influencer marketing — the mechanics.**

- **Tiers by audience size**, each a different trust/reach trade-off: Nano (roughly 1K-10K followers, highest perceived authenticity and engagement rate, lowest cost), Micro (10K-100K, still credible within a niche, cost-efficient), Macro (100K-1M, broad reach, lower per-follower engagement), Celebrity/Mega (1M+, maximum reach, lowest relative trust and engagement, highest cost).
- **Deal structures:** flat sponsorship fee; affiliate/commission-based (pays per resulting sale, aligns incentive but shifts risk to the creator, so it needs a real trackable conversion path); gifting/product-seeding (no cash, relies on genuine advocacy, unpredictable but authentic when it lands); long-term ambassadorships (a recurring relationship builds cumulative trust versus a one-off post that reads as an obvious ad).
- **PESO overlap:** a paid sponsored post is Paid media; the same creator mentioning the product unprompted is Earned; either can be amplified as Shared, and a strong post is often worth accelerating with Paid — influencer marketing isn't a fifth media type, it's a channel that lands in either Paid or Earned depending on whether money changed hands for that specific post (see [content-marketing-peso.md](../marketing/content-marketing-peso.md)).
- **Measurement:** engagement rate, not raw follower count, is the primary quality signal; track via unique promo codes, affiliate links, or UTM parameters for attributable conversions, but treat awareness-stage influencer work more like brand spend (reach, sentiment, reusable UGC) than like a direct-response channel.

**Community-led growth — the mechanics.**

- **What counts as a community, for this purpose:** a persistent space where members interact with *each other*, not just with the brand — a Discord, a user forum, a local meetup series, a certification or ambassador program. A brand's social media following is not a community by this definition; a community exists when members would still show up if the brand stopped posting.
- **Why it compounds:** an active community functions as a distinct kind of loop — members answer each other's questions (reducing support cost), generate UGC and organic advocacy (a content or viral loop input, see [growth-loops.md](../marketing/growth-loops.md)), and create belonging that raises switching costs, a retention effect both Aaker's Brand Loyalty asset and Keller's Resonance apex point to (see [brand-equity-aaker.md](../marketing/brand-equity-aaker.md), [brand-equity-keller-cbbe.md](../marketing/brand-equity-keller-cbbe.md)).
- **Maturity stages:** Seeding (the team manually engages a handful of early members 1:1, no self-sustaining activity yet) → Activation (a critical mass starts engaging with each other unprompted) → Self-sustaining (members onboard, moderate, and answer questions for each other; the team's role shifts from participant to steward) → Advocacy engine (the community actively produces referrals, UGC, and product feedback that feeds the roadmap).
- **Metrics that matter,** distinct from vanity member counts: the ratio of member-to-member interactions versus brand-to-member interactions (a healthy community trends toward the former), response time to a new member's first question from other members, and retention/return rate of community members specifically versus non-member customers.

## Example

A DTC fitness-equipment brand runs both channels distinctly. **Influencer:** partners with fifteen micro-fitness-instructors (10K-50K followers each) on an affiliate deal for a product launch, measured directly against sales through each unique code — treated as a performance-marketing line item. **Community:** separately invests in a private group for existing customers focused on workout accountability and form-check video sharing — a twelve-month build with no direct sales attribution expected in month one. Six months in, the influencer program's ROI is clean and attributable, ranked and optimized like any paid channel; the community shows no direct sales attribution but a measurably higher six-month retention rate among members versus non-members, and its most active members have started making unprompted UGC content the marketing team repurposes as ad creative — an unplanned Earned/Shared output the original community investment thesis didn't explicitly promise but is exactly the compounding behavior the model predicts.

## Applying it for a client

Separate these into different investment cases and different success metrics from the start. The most common client-side mistake is expecting community to deliver influencer-marketing-style, campaign-length, directly attributable ROI, then killing the initiative at month three for "not converting," when community is structurally a twelve-month-plus retention play, not an acquisition campaign. For influencer specifically, push clients away from chasing follower count toward engagement rate, and toward affiliate structures over flat fees whenever the product has a trackable conversion path. For community specifically, insist on a real "would a member show up if we stopped posting" test before recommending the investment — a client wanting community mainly as a cheaper support channel or a broadcast list dressed up as "community" will build a space with low member-to-member interaction and churn the initiative once the novelty wears off.

## Watch-outs

- Confusing a social media following with a community — the retention and compounding effects here only apply to a space with real peer-to-peer interaction; a large follower count with zero member-to-member interaction is a broadcast list, not a community asset.
- Judging influencer marketing purely on follower count rather than engagement rate and audience fit — a mega-influencer with a broad, low-relevance audience frequently underperforms a well-matched micro-influencer on actual conversion, at a fraction of the cost.
- Expecting community investment to show attributable ROI on a campaign timeline — measure it against retention and UGC/referral output, not month-one sales lift, or the initiative gets killed prematurely.
- Influencer relationships built as one-off transactional posts read as obviously paid and generate less trust transfer than long-term ambassador relationships.
- Community requires genuine ongoing stewardship — moderation, seeding discussion, recognizing active members; an unmoderated, unseeded space decays into a ghost channel fast, and reviving a dead community is harder than launching a new one.
