# Competitor Analysis Matrix

A structured, side-by-side comparison of a business against its direct and indirect competitors across the dimensions that actually matter for strategy — not a vague "know your competition" exercise but a specific, fillable table you build once and update continuously.

## When to use

Whenever a client needs to define or defend a Unique Selling Proposition (USP), is deciding on pricing, is entering a market with existing players, or simply can't articulate what makes them different from the three businesses customers keep comparing them to. Do this before STP and before the 4Ps — you can't position against competitors you haven't mapped.

## How it works

**Why do it at all.** Competitor research does three things: (1) identifies the business's own USP by contrast, and whether there's enough unmet demand in the market; (2) shows what competitors are doing right and where they're falling short — continuously monitoring their successes, failures, and investments signals how the market is moving; (3) informs pricing — knowing the competitive landscape helps set a price that maintains a competitive edge. Key message: **competitor research identifies where your competitors are and where you could be.**

**Getting started (5 steps):**

1. **Identify main competitors** — both direct (same product, same customer) and indirect (different product, same customer need).
2. **Analyze their medium** — emphasize online presence (website, social, marketplace listings) since that's usually the most observable and fastest-changing signal.
3. **Gather information** — "wear the consumer's hat": go through their buying journey as a customer would, don't just read their About page.
4. **Track your findings** — data trends over time, not a single snapshot; competitors move.
5. **Check reviews & hunt improvement areas** — customer reviews reveal gaps competitors haven't fixed, which is often the fastest route to a differentiator.

**The matrix itself.** Build one column per competitor (typically 3, but scale as needed) against these rows:

| Competitive Analysis Element |
|---|
| Competitor Identification |
| Target Customer |
| Market Share |
| Product / Service Analysis |
| Strength |
| Weakness |
| Differentiators |
| Partnerships |
| Pricing Strategies (Price Range) |
| Marketing and Advertising Approaches |
| Geography |
| Customer Reviews |
| Distribution Channels |

## Example

A client running a boutique fitness studio in New Cairo builds the matrix against Competitor 1 (a large gym chain), Competitor 2 (a home-workout app), Competitor 3 (a niche pilates studio nearby). Filling in "Pricing Strategies" alone reveals the chain competes on low monthly price with high volume, the app competes on near-zero marginal cost, and the pilates studio charges a premium for specialization — which tells the client their own price has to be justified either by specialization (like the pilates studio) or by a service layer the low-price players can't match (like personal coaching), not by trying to undercut the chain.

## Applying it for a client

Fill the matrix in a workshop with the client rather than handing them a finished document — they usually know competitors intimately but have never organized that knowledge into comparable rows, and the act of filling it in together surfaces the USP faster than presenting conclusions. Once filled, read the matrix row-by-row (not competitor-by-competitor): scan "Differentiators" across all competitors at once to spot the white space, scan "Pricing Strategies" across all to see where the client's price actually needs to sit. That's the direct input into the STP positioning statement (see [stp-model.md](stp-model.md)) and into 4Ps pricing decisions (see [marketing-mix-4ps.md](marketing-mix-4ps.md)).

## Watch-outs

- Indirect competitors get skipped far more often than direct ones — a client fixated on the competitor with the identical product often misses the substitute solving the same customer need in a different form.
- The matrix is a point-in-time artifact; competitors reprice, rebrand, and reposition — revisit it periodically, don't build it once and treat it as permanent truth.
- Don't stop at listing competitor facts — the payoff is in the "Differentiators" row and what it implies about the client's own USP; a matrix with 12 filled rows and no conclusion drawn from it hasn't done its job.
