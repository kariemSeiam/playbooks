# Introduction to Marketing

Marketing is not advertising and it isn't sales — it's the discipline of creating value for a customer and capturing some of that value back, through a deliberate relationship with them. Everything else (STP, the 4Ps, campaigns, channels) is just tooling in service of those two moves.

## When to use

Use this framing whenever a client (or you) conflates marketing with "posting on social media" or "running ads." Reach for it at the very start of any marketing engagement, before touching any tactic — it sets the sequencing for everything downstream and prevents jumping straight to promotion without first understanding the market and the customer.

## How it works

**What marketing is.** The discipline evolved through four historical orientations (Narver & Slater, 1990): Production concept (make it cheap and available) → Quality concept (make it good) → Selling concept (push what you've made) → **Marketing orientation** (find out what's needed, then make and deliver it). Modern marketing practice operates from that last orientation.

**The two elements of "how to get the job."** Whatever else marketing does, it is always doing one of two things:

1. **Creating value** — for the customer, not just for the business. If there's no real value in the offer, no amount of promotion fixes that.
2. **Customer relationship building** — value has to be exchanged and sustained over time, not just delivered once.

**The 5-step marketing process.** This is the operating sequence that ties the rest of the marketing curriculum together — each step is its own body of work:

1. **Understand the Marketplace** — market conditions, competitors, customers (see [understanding-the-marketplace.md](understanding-the-marketplace.md)).
2. **Design the Strategy** — decide who you serve and how (STP — see [stp-model.md](stp-model.md)).
3. **Apply the Strategy** — execute through the marketing mix (4Ps — see [marketing-mix-4ps.md](marketing-mix-4ps.md)).
4. **Customer Relationships** — build relationships that outlast the first sale (see [building-customer-relationships.md](building-customer-relationships.md)).
5. **Capture Value From Customers** — the payoff: revenue, loyalty, market share, advocacy.

Note the ordering discipline: strategy design (step 2) and execution (step 3) only happen *after* the market is understood (step 1). Skipping step 1 is the most common real-world failure mode — a strategy built on assumptions instead of research.

## Example

A founder wants to launch a bottled cold-brew coffee line and says "our marketing is Instagram ads." Run the 5 steps: (1) understand the marketplace — who else sells RTD coffee locally, what do buyers actually complain about; (2) design strategy — pick a segment (e.g., young professionals who skip breakfast) and decide how to position against Nescafé Frappé and artisanal coffee shops; (3) apply strategy — set price, packaging, distribution (convenience stores vs. e-commerce), and only then promotion; (4) build relationships — subscription/refill perks, community; (5) capture value — repeat purchase rate, average order value. The Instagram ad is one line inside step 3, not the whole plan.

## Applying it for a client

Use the 5-step process as a diagnostic before any tactical conversation: ask the client which step they're actually stuck on. Most clients arrive at step 3 (execution) wanting tactics, when the real gap is step 1 (they don't actually know their market) or step 2 (they haven't chosen who they're for). Naming the gap out loud — "you're asking for a promotion plan but you haven't picked a target segment yet" — reframes the engagement and stops premature tactical work. It also gives you a natural table of contents for a marketing audit or a strategy deck.

## Watch-outs

- Treating "marketing" as synonymous with "promotion" (the 4th P) is the single most common client misconception — correct it early, explicitly.
- Value creation without relationship building produces one-time transactions, not a business; relationship building on top of a weak value proposition produces churn dressed up as loyalty programs.
- The 5 steps look linear but in practice are cyclical — captured value (step 5) and relationship feedback (step 4) should feed back into re-understanding the marketplace (step 1).
