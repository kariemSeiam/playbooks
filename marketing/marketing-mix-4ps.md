# Marketing Mix (4Ps)

The 4Ps are the four executional levers a business pulls to actually deliver on the position chosen in STP — Product, Price, Place, Promotion. Where STP decides who you serve and what image you occupy, the 4Ps decide the concrete, operational choices that make that position real to the customer.

## When to use

Only after STP is settled (see [stp-model.md](stp-model.md)) — the 4Ps are the "Apply the Strategy" step of the marketing process (see [marketing-strategy-overview.md](marketing-strategy-overview.md)), not a substitute for deciding who the target is. Use it as the working checklist any time a client is finalizing a launch plan, a repricing decision, a distribution choice, or a campaign — it's the single-page structure that makes sure none of the four gets decided in isolation from the others.

## How it works

**1st P — Product.** What is actually being offered, decided across four elements:

- **Product Design** — form, function, how it's engineered/built.
- **Product Description** — how it's explained, the claims made about it.
- **Product Packaging** — physical or digital wrapping, first-touch impression.
- **Product Features** — the specific capabilities included.
(Broader dimensions referenced in the mix overview: features, quality, branding, packaging, guarantee.)

**2nd P — Place.** Where and how the product reaches the customer. Four questions to work through:

1. Where will potential customers search for this product?
2. Is physical interaction with the product necessary before purchase?
3. Will the sales strategy focus on direct marketing through the business's own e-commerce platform, or do customers typically find the business on third-party marketplaces?
4. Does the business prefer direct customer interaction during purchases, or is outsourcing customer service to a third-party provider being considered?
(Broader dimensions: channels, locations, inventory, transport, market coverage.)

**3rd P — Price.** What the business charges, balancing two constraints: it has to be a fair deal the customer is willing to pay for the value received, while also covering costs and generating profit so the business can keep improving and serving customers better.

*Objectives of pricing* — every pricing decision should be traceable to one of three goals: **Market Continuity** (staying viable/competitive over time), **Increasing Profit Margin**, **Controlling the Market** (using price as a competitive weapon, e.g. to block entrants or gain share).

*Pricing strategies* (nine named options, choose based on the pricing objective and the STP position):

- **Penetration Pricing** — enter low to build share/volume fast.
- **Market Skimming** — enter high, then lower price over time to capture willingness-to-pay tiers.
- **Premium Pricing** — sustained high price to signal quality/exclusivity.
- **Cost Plus Pricing** — price = cost + fixed margin.
- **Psychological Pricing** — pricing that leverages perception (e.g., charm pricing, price anchoring).
- **Economy Pricing** — consistently low price, low-frills, low marketing spend.
- **Dynamic Pricing** — price fluctuates with demand, timing, or customer segment in real time.
- **Competitive Pricing** — price set directly relative to competitors' prices.
- **Value Based Pricing** — price set according to the value the customer perceives/receives, not according to cost.

**4th P — Promotion.** Communication strategies employed to make potential customers aware of, and attracted to, the product. Three components: **Advertising**, **Public Relations**, **Sales Promotion**.

**Advantages and disadvantages of the 4Ps model itself** (worth knowing before leaning on it too hard):

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| Helps understand what the product can offer the customer | No built-in mechanism to measure success |
| Begins with aligning the product with the customer's wants | Price is treated as fundamental, but brand building isn't represented |
| Shows the critical components of even a complex marketing plan on a single page | High-level — doesn't force operational depth |
| | Might take time to work through properly despite looking simple |

## Example

A startup selling artisanal cold-pressed juice, positioned (via STP) as a premium, health-conscious brand for urban professionals: **Product** — glass bottles (not plastic), minimalist branding signaling purity, short ingredient list as a feature; **Place** — direct-to-consumer via own app plus a curated presence in premium gyms and co-working spaces, not mass retail; **Price** — Premium Pricing strategy, consistent with the STP position, objective being Market Continuity through brand loyalty rather than Controlling the Market through volume; **Promotion** — Public Relations (wellness-influencer partnerships) and targeted digital Advertising, deliberately skipping mass discount-driven Sales Promotion since heavy discounting would contradict the premium position.

## Applying it for a client

Walk the 4Ps in order but check each one against the STP position before finalizing it — the fastest diagnostic for a "confused brand" is a mismatch between one P and the rest (e.g., premium Product + Economy Pricing, or a niche Positioning + mass-market Place). When a client asks specifically "what price should I charge," don't answer with a number — walk them through the Objectives of Pricing first (which of the three are they actually optimizing for), then pick the pricing strategy that serves that objective and fits the STP position, and only then discuss a number. Use the disadvantages column explicitly with clients who want the 4Ps to double as a success dashboard — it wasn't built to measure outcomes, only to structure the plan; pair it with real KPIs from the "Capture Value" step of the marketing process.

## 2024→2026 shift

Generative AI is reshaping the Product and Promotion levers specifically, through two mechanisms:

- **Agentic content-generation and personalization pipelines.** Instead of a single AI-written draft reviewed by a human, agentic pipelines now chain multiple AI steps — generate, brand-check, localize, resize per channel — to produce and personalize creative at a volume and speed the classic Promotion workflow couldn't match. McKinsey's own CMO surveys put roughly 90% of CMOs experimenting with generative AI in marketing, but fewer than 10% report having captured its value end-to-end — the gap sits in governance, brand-guardrail enforcement, and integrating AI output into an actual production pipeline rather than a novelty demo.
- **Dynamic Creative Optimization (DCO).** AI assembles and tests ad variants (headline, image, offer, CTA) in real time against live performance data, rather than a media team pre-building a fixed set of creative variants for a static A/B test. This turns Promotion from a periodic "we ship a campaign" motion into a continuously self-optimizing one, but it also means creative decisions are increasingly made by the platform's optimization algorithm, not the marketer, raising the same brand-consistency risk the McKinsey gap points to.

This shift is emerging-but-credible, not yet a settled practice: the technology is real and already in production at scale for some categories (Dynamic Creative Optimization in particular is mature inside ad platforms), but the McKinsey figure shows most organizations are still failing to operationalize it past a pilot. Treat a client's "we use AI for content now" claim as a starting point for due diligence, not a solved problem — ask specifically what fraction of the funnel from generation to brand-approved, published, measured output is actually automated versus still manually patched together.

## Watch-outs

- Setting price by Cost Plus alone because it's the easiest to compute, when the STP position calls for Value Based or Premium pricing — cost-plus systematically underprices a strong value proposition.
- Treating Promotion as the whole of "marketing" (the most common client conflation, also flagged in [intro-to-marketing.md](intro-to-marketing.md)) — it's one of four levers, and often not even the one doing the most work.
- Deciding Place based on convenience (whatever channel is easiest to set up) rather than on where the target segment from STP actually searches and buys.
- Using the 4Ps model as if it measures success — per its own listed disadvantages, it has no built-in success metric; don't present "we did the 4Ps" as equivalent to "the plan is working."
