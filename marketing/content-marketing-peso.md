# PESO Model (Paid, Earned, Shared, Owned Media)

A framework created by PR strategist Gini Dietrich that classifies every marketing and communications channel into four media types — Paid, Earned, Shared, Owned — and, more importantly, shows how the four integrate and reinforce each other rather than operating as separate departmental silos (PR running Earned, ads running Paid, social running Shared, content running Owned, rarely talking to each other). It's the standard taxonomy for organizing a content and channel strategy across what used to be treated as entirely separate disciplines.

## When to use

- A client's marketing or comms function is organizationally siloed — a PR team, an ads team, a social team, a content team that don't coordinate — and campaigns feel disjointed as a result
- Building a channel or media plan from scratch, needing a shared vocabulary not biased toward one discipline
- Diagnosing why paid spend isn't converting as well as expected — often an Owned/Earned credibility gap Paid spend alone can't fix
- Explaining to a client the difference between bought reach (Paid) and earned trust (Earned), and why a plan needs both
- Structuring a content calendar or production plan across channel types instead of by channel name alone

## How it works

**The four media types:**

| Type | Definition | Examples | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Paid** | Media placement the business pays for directly | Search ads, social ads, display, sponsorships, paid influencer partnerships | Speed and control — the only type where reach and timing are directly purchasable |
| **Earned** | Coverage or mentions gained through PR, relationships, or newsworthiness, not paid directly | Press coverage, organic reviews, analyst mentions, unpaid influencer mentions, word of mouth | Third-party credibility Paid media structurally cannot buy |
| **Shared** | Content distributed via social platforms and amplified by others' engagement | Organic social posts, shares, comments, reshares, community discussion | Amplification at near-zero marginal cost, though algorithm-dependent |
| **Owned** | Channels or content the business fully controls | Website, blog, email list, app, branded podcast or channel | Long-term asset-building and message control, immune to a platform's algorithm change |

**How the four integrate — the model's actual point.** Dietrich's core argument is that these four types are a single reinforcing system, not four separate campaigns run by four separate teams:

- **Owned** content is the substrate — what gets pointed to, cited, and built upon by everything else.
- **Earned** coverage lends third-party credibility to that Owned content and drives traffic Paid alone can't buy, since an ad is discounted by the audience as self-interested while a news mention isn't.
- **Shared** activity amplifies both the Owned content and the Earned coverage across social networks at near-zero cost, and generates the social-proof signals that reinforce Earned credibility further.
- **Paid** media then targets and accelerates all three — boosting a high-performing organic post, promoting Earned coverage to a wider audience than it reached organically, or directing paid traffic straight to the Owned asset to control the destination experience.

The failure mode this integration prevents: a PR team lands a great Earned placement but no one boosts it with Paid or links it into Owned content, so its reach and lifespan is limited to whoever saw it that day; or an ads team runs a strong Paid campaign into a thin Owned landing page with no real content behind it, wasting the paid spend's credibility-building potential.

**Sequencing a plan.** Build Owned first — the durable foundation everything else points back to, and the one asset type the client fully controls — then generate Earned coverage of that Owned content, then seed and amplify via Shared, then accelerate the winners with Paid, boosting what's already proving to work organically rather than paying to promote unproven content cold.

## Example

A B2B fintech client publishes original research on SME lending trends (**Owned** — hosted on the site, gated for lead capture). A PR push secures coverage in two regional business publications citing the report's data (**Earned** — third-party credibility no ad could buy). The social team clips the report's key stats into shareable graphics and a founder LinkedIn post, reshared organically by industry contacts (**Shared** — amplification at near-zero cost). Seeing which social post and which press mention are getting the most engagement, the client puts a modest paid budget behind boosting that specific post and retargeting visitors who read the press coverage but didn't download the report (**Paid** — accelerating what's already proven, not guessing cold). The four types reinforce a single narrative and a single Owned destination, rather than four disconnected campaigns.

## Applying it for a client

Use the PESO grid as a structural audit before proposing a content calendar: categorize every existing initiative into one of the four boxes and check for gaps. The single most common finding is a client heavy in Paid and Shared but with a thin or outdated Owned asset base to point traffic toward, or Earned treated as a wholly separate PR function that never gets amplified into the other three. Recommend building or refreshing the Owned foundation first for any client whose plan starts with "let's run some ads" or "let's get some press" without a strong destination asset behind either. For clients with siloed teams — a PR agency, a separate paid-media agency, an in-house social person — use the PESO model explicitly as the shared brief that forces coordination around one Owned asset and one narrative, rather than each optimizing their own channel type in isolation.

## Watch-outs

- Treating the four types as four separate campaigns run by four separate teams, the most common failure, defeats the model's entire purpose — the point is integration, not classification for its own sake.
- Paid media pointed at a thin or outdated Owned asset wastes the credibility-transfer effect the model relies on — check the landing destination's quality before recommending a paid push.
- Earned media is the hardest type to predict or guarantee — don't build a plan's timeline around an assumed press hit landing on schedule; treat Earned as an accelerant when it lands, not a plannable, on-demand input like Paid.
- Shared/social performance is algorithm-dependent and can shift overnight — an Owned asset is the only type immune to a platform decision, which is the argument for prioritizing Owned even though it's the slowest to build.
