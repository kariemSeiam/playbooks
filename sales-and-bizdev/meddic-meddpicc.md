# MEDDIC / MEDDPICC

MEDDIC is an acronym-based qualification checklist for complex B2B deals: Metrics, Economic buyer, Decision criteria, Decision process, Identify pain, Champion. It matters to a consultant because it's not a discovery script — it's a diagnostic for whether a deal a rep calls "on track" is actually real, or whether the rep has been talking to the wrong person, chasing a wish rather than a budgeted pain, or mistaking a helpful coach for a champion who can actually move the deal. Dick Dunkel built it at PTC (Parametric Technology Corporation) in 1996, working under SVP of Sales John McMahon and in collaboration with colleague Jack Napoli, after the sales org identified that deal outcomes traced reliably back to the same six gaps in information or access. Using it, PTC more than tripled revenue from roughly $300M to $1B in four years. McMahon carried the framework to later companies and boards, and it has since become the dominant qualification standard at high-growth enterprise B2B and SaaS sales organizations — extended by practitioners into **MEDDPICC** (adding Paper process and Competition) as deal complexity and competitive pressure grew.

## When to use

- Running deal reviews/forecast calls for enterprise or complex B2B pipeline, where "will this close" needs an evidence-based answer, not a rep's gut feel.
- Coaching a rep on a stalled or ambiguous deal — MEDDPICC pinpoints exactly which piece of information or access is missing.
- Redesigning CRM opportunity fields and stage-exit criteria so that advancing a deal stage requires real qualification data, not just a rep's click.
- Diagnosing why forecasted deals keep slipping or going to "no decision" — usually traceable to a specific missing letter (most often Economic Buyer or Champion).
- Onboarding new enterprise AEs — MEDDPICC gives them a checklist for what "fully qualified" actually means before they over-invest in a deal.

## How it works

Each letter is a category of information the rep must actively establish, not assume. MEDDIC is the original six; MEDDPICC adds Paper Process and Competition for orgs with heavier procurement/legal cycles and more competitive deals.

| Letter | Element | Core question | Red flag if missing |
|---|---|---|---|
| M | Metrics | What quantifiable business outcome does the buyer expect (revenue gain, cost cut, hours saved, risk reduced), stated in their numbers, not yours? | Rep can only describe pain qualitatively ("they're frustrated") with no number attached — deal has no ROI story to survive a budget challenge. |
| E | Economic Buyer | Who has actual authority to release the money — not who the rep talks to most? | Rep has only met champions/influencers, never been in a room with the actual budget owner. |
| D | Decision Criteria | What formal and informal checklist will the buying committee score vendors against (technical, financial, relationship)? | Rep is guessing at criteria instead of having seen or helped shape a written scorecard/RFP. |
| D | Decision Process | What are the concrete steps, approvals, and stakeholders (security review, legal, procurement, board sign-off) between now and signature, with dates? | Rep has a single "close date" with no map of the steps that have to happen before it. |
| P | Paper Process | What does contracting actually require — MSA redlines, security questionnaires, procurement portal, budget cycle timing? | Deal is "verbally agreed" but legal/security hasn't started, which routinely adds weeks the forecast doesn't account for. |
| I | Identify Pain | Is there a compelling, business-level reason to act now, distinct from a nice-to-have? | Rep describes interest but can't state what breaks or what it costs the buyer if nothing changes. |
| C | Champion | Is there an internal person with real influence who wants the rep to win and will sell on their behalf when they're not in the room? | The only internal contact is a "coach" (gives information) rather than a champion (spends political capital to advance the deal). |
| C | Competition | What else is being considered — including "do nothing," internal build, and the incumbent — and how does the buyer perceive the alternatives? | Rep only tracks named competitor vendors and has no read on status-quo inertia, which is the most common deal-killer in enterprise sales. |

Deal reviews typically score each letter red/yellow/green (or 0–2). A deal shouldn't be forecast as committed unless Economic Buyer, Champion, and Decision Process are all green — those three are the load-bearing elements; the rest can be filled in as the deal progresses.

## Example

A consultancy is coaching a SaaS client's enterprise team on a $180K ARR logistics-software deal the rep has forecast to close this quarter. Running it through MEDDPICC in the deal review surfaces: Metrics — captured (client believes the tool saves 12 minutes per dispatch, ~$240K/year at their volume). Economic Buyer — **not confirmed**; the rep has only met the Director of Operations, who influences but doesn't sign contracts above $100K (that requires the COO). Champion — the Director is enthusiastic but hasn't yet introduced the rep to anyone else, a coach-not-champion pattern. Decision Process — unclear; no timeline for security review, which this buyer's industry (logistics, handling customer PII) always requires. Paper Process — unstarted. Competition — the real competitor is the client's current spreadsheet-based process, not another vendor. The review reclassifies the deal from "Commit" to "Best Case" and generates two concrete next actions: get the Director to broker a COO meeting, and ask directly whether a security review is required.

## Applying it for a client

If brought in to audit or fix a sales org, don't just teach the acronym — walk the *entire open pipeline* through MEDDPICC in one sitting and tag each deal's weakest letter. That single exercise usually reveals a systemic pattern (e.g., every deal is missing Economic Buyer because reps default to selling to the most responsive stakeholder, who is rarely the signer) that's more valuable to fix than any individual deal. Rebuild the CRM opportunity object so each MEDDPICC letter is a required field with a note, not a checkbox, and make stage advancement conditional on specific letters being filled — e.g., a deal can't move to "Proposal" without a named Economic Buyer. Redesign the deal-review meeting itself around MEDDPICC gaps rather than stage-and-close-date, since stage-based reviews reward optimistic rounding while gap-based reviews force the rep to say what they don't know yet.

## Watch-outs

- Champion vs. coach is the single most common confusion — a coach gives you information, a champion spends their own political capital to get you the win. Reps routinely mislabel a friendly, talkative contact as a champion.
- The Economic Buyer is often not the highest title the rep has met — a VP can be enthusiastic and still lack authority to release the specific budget line. Confirm authority over *this specific purchase*, not general seniority.
- MEDDPICC can degenerate into theater — reps fill every CRM field with a plausible-sounding answer without having actually earned that access from the buyer. A manager who accepts field completion as proof of qualification, rather than probing how the rep knows each answer, gets fooled by their own process.
- It's a qualification and deal-tracking framework, not a questioning technique — it tells you *what* you need to know, not *how* to surface it in conversation (see [spin-selling.md](spin-selling.md) for that layer).
- Doesn't fit transactional, low-touch, or product-led motions with no sustained human sales relationship — applying full MEDDPICC to a self-serve deal under a few thousand dollars is overhead the deal size can't support (see [bant-and-spiced.md](bant-and-spiced.md) for lighter-weight alternatives).
